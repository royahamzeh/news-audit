China warns it will protect its interests after US widens sanctions against Iran

China has issued a warning to protect its national interests following the United States' decision to widen sanctions against Iran. The move by the US aims to further isolate countries that continue business relations with Tehran, significantly affecting China, as it is a major purchaser of Iranian oil. The US sanctions are a result of Iran's ongoing nuclear activities that Washington believes are a threat to global security. China's Foreign Ministry spokesperson stated that the sanctions are unilateral and called for an end to the so-called 'long-arm jurisdiction' of the US. Beijing has historically supported Iran in the face of US sanctions and is likely to maintain its economic ties despite the pressure. This situation creates potential friction between China and the United States, both of which hold significant influence on the global stage. As the world's second-largest economy, China's stance on international sanctions has wide-reaching implications, especially given its Belt and Road Initiative that often partners with countries under US sanctions. The increased tension arrives at a moment when US-China relations are already strained over several issues, including trade and human rights. The outcome of these developments remains uncertain as both nations navigate the complex geopolitical landscape.
