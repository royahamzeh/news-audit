Powerful 7.7-magnitude earthquake kills at least 38 in Indonesia

A devastating 7.7-magnitude earthquake struck Flores, an island in eastern Indonesia, claiming the lives of at least 38 people. The powerful quake caused widespread destruction, leaving rescue teams scrambling to find survivors amid the rubble. The seismic event, which struck early in the morning, was followed by numerous aftershocks, complicating rescue efforts. Buildings were flattened, roads cracked, and critical infrastructure was severely damaged, cutting off certain areas from aid. The Indonesian government has dispatched emergency response teams to the region to assist in search and rescue operations. Meanwhile, hospitals are overwhelmed with the injured, and makeshift medical facilities have been set up to accommodate the increasing number of casualties. Survivors have taken refuge in temporary shelters set up by local authorities as tremors continue to rattle the region. The National Disaster Mitigation Agency is coordinating efforts to provide relief and support to the affected communities. The earthquake is a grim reminder of Indonesia's vulnerability to seismic activity due to its position on the Pacific Ring of Fire, a zone of high volcanic and earthquake activity. As search and rescue operations continue, authorities are urging residents to remain vigilant and prepared for potential aftershocks.
