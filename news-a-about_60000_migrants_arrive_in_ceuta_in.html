About 60,000 migrants arrive in Ceuta in 24 hours, Spanish territory's leader says

In an unprecedented event, the autonomous Spanish territory of Ceuta has seen an influx of about 60,000 migrants in a span of just 24 hours, according to the territory's leader. This sudden surge in arrivals is staggering compared to the population of Ceuta, which stands at around 83,600 people. Such a significant increase in numbers has raised alarm and concern not just within Ceuta but also across mainland Spain and the European Union. The migrants, who are believed to originate from various African nations, took advantage of the relatively open border between Morocco and Ceuta. This incident has put immense pressure on local authorities, who are struggling to provide adequate shelter and resources to those who have crossed over. The Spanish government is reportedly in urgent talks with the European Union to seek assistance in managing the situation. Ceuta's leader has described the event as an unprecedented humanitarian challenge and has called for immediate international cooperation to address this crisis. The situation underscores the ongoing challenges in managing migration flows along the Mediterranean and the necessity for coordinated international efforts to find sustainable solutions for displaced populations.
