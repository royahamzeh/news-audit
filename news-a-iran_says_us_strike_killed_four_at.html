Iran says US strike killed four at wedding in 'war crime' as US denies targeting civilians

In a dramatic escalation of tensions, Iranian officials have accused the United States of committing a 'war crime' following an airstrike that allegedly killed four people, including two children, at a wedding in the Middle East. Iranian media sources reported that shrapnel from the strike hit a residence where a wedding ceremony was underway. The tragic incident has provoked outrage, with Iran responding by launching a series of missile and drone attacks on US military assets in the region. The United States has denied any deliberate targeting of civilians, emphasizing that their operations adhere to international law and that they take extensive measures to avoid civilian casualties. The Pentagon released a statement asserting that the strike was aimed at neutralizing legitimate military threats and expressed condolences for any loss of life, underscoring their commitment to investigating the incident thoroughly. Meanwhile, diplomatic relations have further soured, with Iran's government vowing retaliation and calling for international condemnation of the US actions. While the region remains tense, global leaders are urging restraint and dialogue to prevent further escalation. This incident is likely to galvanize calls for de-escalation in conflict zones and raise questions about military engagement rules in densely populated areas.
