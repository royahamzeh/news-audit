Germany's Merz attacks AfD in stormy debate after far-right election win

In a passionate and stormy debate in the German parliament, Chancellor Friedrich Merz launched a fierce attack on the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) party following their recent election victory. The contentious exchange was sparked by the AfD’s controversial stance on migration policies. Merz took a firm stand against AfD leader Alice Weidel, accusing her party’s migration policy of effectively promoting 'ethnic cleansing' of the skilled workforce in Germany. Merz argued that the AfD’s approach to immigration is detrimental to the nation’s economy, which relies heavily on a diverse pool of skilled laborers. During the heated discussion, Merz highlighted the potential risks posed to Germany’s social and economic fabric should such policies gain wider acceptance. Alice Weidel, on her part, defended her party’s position, arguing that the AfD seeks to prioritize German citizens' employment opportunities and national security. The debate underscored the growing polarization in Germany’s political landscape, with the AfD gaining noticeable traction among voters disillusioned with the mainstream parties. The election win is seen as a significant advancement for the AfD, intensifying the challenges faced by other German political parties in containing the far-right’s ascent in Germany’s body politic, as they compete for the support of an increasingly divided electorate.
