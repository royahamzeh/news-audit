Trump says Iran will be 'hit very hard' if Hormuz Strait not open soon as oil prices fall

Tensions in the Middle East have once again peaked following former President Donald Trump's stark ultimatum to Iran. In a recent statement, Trump declared that Iran would be 'hit very hard' if they didn't comply with demands to keep the strategically critical Hormuz Strait open. This crucial chokepoint for global oil shipments has seen tumultuous periods where tensions have resulted in severe disruptions to the oil supply chain. The pressure from the US has come at a time when oil prices have been declining, exacerbating the global economic scenario. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent have stepped into the fray, announcing that diplomatic talks with Iran have progressed significantly. Their efforts are aimed at ensuring the passage through the strait remains uninterrupted, allowing shipments to resume normally. The Strait of Hormuz is pivotal for the transportation of nearly a fifth of the world's oil supply, and any blockage can trigger a ripple effect on global markets. While tensions persist, the recent developments in negotiations bring a glimmer of hope that the situation can be resolved diplomatically, without further escalation. The international community remains anxiously observant, hoping for a peaceful resolution to these heightened tensions.
