Spain's PM blames traffickers after 60,000 migrants reach Ceuta from Morocco

Spain's Prime Minister has expressed grave concerns over the recent surge in illegal migrants arriving in Ceuta, a Spanish territory located in North Africa. Referring to the situation as an "attack" on the region, he has placed the blame squarely on human traffickers, who are believed to be orchestrating these movements. The recent influx has seen an estimated 60,000 migrants crossing from neighboring Morocco into Ceuta, an unprecedented number that has raised alarms within both local and national governments. The Prime Minister has pledged a robust response, declaring that all individuals who have entered Ceuta illegally will be systematically returned to Morocco, their country of origin. This decision underscores Spain's commitment to upholding the integrity of its borders and maintaining order in its territories. The Spanish government is calling for increased international cooperation to combat human trafficking and mitigate the factors driving mass migration. Human rights organizations, however, have urged the government to handle the situation with care, emphasizing the need for humanitarian aid and legal pathways to asylum. As tensions simmer, the ongoing crisis in Ceuta highlights the broader challenges Europe faces in managing migration and calls for a balanced approach that respects both national security and human dignity.
