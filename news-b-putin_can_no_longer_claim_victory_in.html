Putin can no longer claim victory in Ukraine, Nobel Peace Prize winner tells BBC

Nobel Laureate Dmitry Muratov on Putin's Campaign

In an interview with the BBC’s Steve Rosenberg, Nobel Peace Prize winner Dmitry Muratov provided a critical evaluation of the ongoing conflict in Ukraine. According to Muratov, Russian President Vladimir Putin’s ambitions in Ukraine are no longer achievable. Instead of achieving a military victory, Putin might succeed only in "destroying Ukraine, not conquering it," as the conflict stretches into its fifth year.

Key Points

  • The war in Ukraine has entered its fifth year.
  • Muratov claims Putin cannot claim victory anymore.
  • The war has caused severe devastation rather than strategic success.
  • The conflict highlights the need for diplomatic intervention.

Impact and Implications

The protracted conflict in Ukraine, exacerbated by global sanctions and fierce resistance from Ukrainian forces, underscores a significant miscalculation by the Kremlin. Muratov’s insights point to a critical need for peaceful resolutions to prevent further loss and devastation.
