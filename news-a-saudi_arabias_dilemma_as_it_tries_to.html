Saudi Arabia's dilemma as it tries to stay out of US-Iran war

Saudi Arabia is currently grappling with a challenging predicament as tensions between the United States and Iran continue to simmer. The kingdom finds itself at a crossroads: should it maintain its defensive posture and strike back against perceived threats as a deterrent, or should it work towards de-escalating the situation to preserve regional stability? Saudi Arabia has historically been a key ally of the United States, and its alignment with American foreign policy objectives has been a cornerstone of its strategy. However, the ongoing tensions with Iran present a complex challenge. On one hand, retaliating against provocations from Iran can serve as a powerful deterrent and reaffirm Saudi Arabia's commitment to its allies. On the other hand, engaging in a cycle of retaliation could exacerbate tensions, leading to a full-blown conflict that would be detrimental to the region. The Saudi leadership is aware of the potential repercussions of either choice. Therefore, they are exploring diplomatic channels to ease tensions while simultaneously preparing their military forces to defend against any eventual aggression. The kingdom's decision could have significant implications not only for Saudi Arabia but also for regional peace and global energy markets, given their pivotal role as a major oil exporter.
