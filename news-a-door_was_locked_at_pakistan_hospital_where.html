Door was locked at Pakistan hospital where fire killed 14 babies, witnesses say

In a recent tragedy in a Pakistani hospital, a fire broke out in a neonatal ward resulting in the devastating death of 14 infants. The incident took place in the early hours of the morning, and witnesses have claimed that the circumstances surrounding the incident may have been preventable. According to several witnesses, the door to the ward was locked, complicating evacuation efforts during the emergency. However, a hospital official refutes this claim, stating that the door was not locked but rather secured by security guards to ensure the babies' safety. This discrepancy in reports has sparked public outrage and raised questions about the hospital's safety protocols. Emergency responders arrived on the scene quickly, but the flames and smoke had already taken a tragic toll. Bereaved families are now demanding accountability and a thorough investigation to understand the potential lapses that led to this massive loss of life. The hospital authorities have expressed their condolences and commitment to cooperating fully with any inquiries. Safety regulations and emergency procedures in medical facilities are now under scrutiny, as the public and government officials call for reforms to prevent such incidents in the future. The tragedy has highlighted the need for comprehensive safety measures to protect the most vulnerable patients.
