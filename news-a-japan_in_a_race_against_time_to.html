Japan in a ‘race against time’ to rescue people trapped beneath rubble after quake

Japan is facing an urgent crisis as rescue teams race against time to save individuals trapped beneath the rubble caused by a devastating earthquake on the island of Kyushu. The earthquake struck on Tuesday, and despite the swift response of emergency services, at least 13 people have been confirmed dead. The quake has left a trail of destruction, with numerous buildings reduced to piles of debris and infrastructure severely impacted. Rescuers are working fervently amidst aftershocks, which pose an ongoing threat to already unstable structures. The government has deployed the Self-Defense Forces to assist in the relief operations, with hundreds of volunteers also joining the effort. Emergency services are utilizing heavy machinery and specially trained dogs to search for survivors. Families anxiously await news about their missing loved ones as rescue workers battle against time and the elements. Authorities have set up evacuation centers, providing temporary shelter and basic necessities for those displaced. Prime Minister Fumio Kishida has expressed deep sorrow over the tragedy and assured the nation that all necessary resources will be mobilized to aid rescue and relief operations. As night falls, the urgency of the operation intensifies, with hopes of finding more survivors dwindling by the hour.
