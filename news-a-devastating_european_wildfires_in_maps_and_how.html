Devastating European wildfires in maps - and how they're being tackled

The wildfires sweeping across Europe this summer have caused unprecedented damage, particularly near Bordeaux in France and throughout the Madrid region of Spain. In less than four days, a ferocious blaze near Bordeaux has ravaged over 162 square miles (420 square kilometers), consuming vast tracts of land and threatening local communities. Efforts to control the fires have been hampered by high temperatures and strong winds, creating a challenging environment for firefighters. In Spain, the Madrid region is experiencing one of its worst fire seasons in history. The blazes have scorched large areas, resulting in evacuations and significant disruptions to daily life. Authorities, along with emergency services, are working tirelessly to contain the fires and mitigate the impact on residents. The European Union has dispatched additional resources, including water-bombing aircraft and fire suppression specialists, to aid local efforts. In response to these devastating fires, governments are also emphasizing the need for long-term strategies to address climate change, which is fueling such extreme weather events. Improved forest management practices and increased investment in firefighting technologies are being considered to prevent future outbreaks. As Europe battles these relentless wildfires, the priority remains the safety of citizens and the protection of natural environments.
