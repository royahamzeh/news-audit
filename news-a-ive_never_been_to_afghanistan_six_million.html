'I've never been to Afghanistan': Six million deportees forced to start over under the Taliban

Millions of people who have lived their entire lives in Pakistan and Iran find themselves being forced back to Afghanistan, a country many have never set foot in. As of August, an additional million asylum-seekers and migrants were deported to Afghanistan this year, taking the total to an alarming six million under the current Taliban regime. The returnees face monumental challenges because they are essentially strangers in their homeland. Distrust and lack of familiar support networks exacerbate the crisis as many struggle to reintegrate into Afghan society. These individuals and families are trying to carve out a new life in a country that has changed dramatically from what their parents and grandparents remember. Many have never called Afghanistan home, complicating their sense of identity and belonging. With limited job opportunities, perpetual insecurity, and ongoing economic challenges under the Taliban, their future remains fraught with uncertainty. International aid organizations are stepping in to provide basic humanitarian assistance, but resources are stretched thin. The urgency to develop a sustainable plan to reintegrate these returnees into Afghan society is growing by the day. Without viable strategies, Afghanistan risks losing a demographic that could potentially contribute to rebuilding its future.
