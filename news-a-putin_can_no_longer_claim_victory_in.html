Putin can no longer claim victory in Ukraine, Nobel Peace Prize winner tells BBC

In a revealing interview with the BBC’s Steve Rosenberg, Nobel Peace Prize winner Dmitry Muratov shared his insights on the ongoing conflict in Ukraine. Muratov, known for his vocal criticism of authoritarian regimes and defense of press freedom, delivered a stark assessment of Russian President Vladimir Putin's military campaign, which has persisted for five grueling years. Muratov asserted that Putin’s objectives in Ukraine have been overwhelmingly thwarted, to the point where victory is now unattainable for the Kremlin leader. Instead, Muratov believes that Putin can only aim to "destroy Ukraine, not conquer it," underscoring the futility and destructiveness of the prolonged conflict. The war, which began as an aggressive territorial grab, has devolved into a bloody stalemate with catastrophic consequences on both sides. Continuous resistance from Ukrainian forces and resilient global sanctions have compounded Russia’s strategic miscalculations, backing Putin into a corner with limited options. As the world witnesses an alarming humanitarian crisis unfold, Muratov's remarks emphasize the urgent need for diplomatic resolution. His insights contribute to the broader discourse on how international power dynamics and relentless warfare perpetuate suffering, highlighting the human cost of global ambitions and the pressing necessity for global leaders to pursue sustainable peace.
