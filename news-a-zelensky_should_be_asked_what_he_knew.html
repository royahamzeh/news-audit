Zelensky should be asked what he knew about government corruption, sacked minister tells BBC

In a startling revelation, a former Ukrainian minister Mykhailo Fedorov has called for more transparency by asking President Volodymyr Zelensky to explain what he knew about government corruption. The statement was made during an interview with the BBC, where Fedorov emphasized the need to address these issues amid the ongoing conflict with Russia. Fedorov also highlighted the importance of holding wartime elections in Ukraine to ensure the country's democratic processes continue to function even during these turbulent times. The call for elections is seen as a move to bolster accountability and maintain public trust in the government. He believes that elections could serve as a vital mechanism to bring new leadership and ideas to the forefront, potentially aiding Ukraine in its fight against systemic corruption. This development could put pressure on Zelensky, who has built his political career on promises to fight corruption and bring about meaningful reform. However, the logistics of conducting elections during a war pose significant challenges, and the impact on the Ukrainian political landscape remains uncertain. This interview underscores the growing internal political pressures Zelensky faces as he navigates both international crises and the demands for internal reform.
