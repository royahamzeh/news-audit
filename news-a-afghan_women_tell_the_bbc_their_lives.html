Afghan women tell the BBC their lives are unrecognisable after five years of Taliban rule

After five years of Taliban rule, Afghan women are facing a dramatically altered reality that has transformed their lives beyond recognition. The Taliban's return to power has been marked by an increase in public floggings, severe restrictions on women's employment, and significant barriers to accessing healthcare, which have collectively endangered countless lives. Women from various parts of the country have come forward to describe their harrowing experiences under the Taliban regime. Many have lost jobs that once provided financial independence and a sense of purpose. The education of girls has taken a severe hit, as schools and universities have seen closures aimed at curtailing female participation in learning. Healthcare access, a critical concern, has been severely impeded by Taliban policies, leaving women especially vulnerable during childbirth and other medical emergencies. Public punishments, such as floggings, have instilled a climate of fear, as they are often meted out for supposed violations of dress codes and other restrictive edicts. Afghan women's narratives collectively paint a picture of a society where personal freedoms have been dramatically curtailed, and the hope for progress and equality seems like a distant memory. The international community continues to watch with concern, as Afghan women call for their stories to be heard.
