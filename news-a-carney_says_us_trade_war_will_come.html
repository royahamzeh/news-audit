Carney says US trade war 'will come at a cost' as Canada strikes back on tariffs

Bank of England Governor Mark Carney has voiced concerns over the escalating trade tensions between the United States and other nations, highlighting that such disputes 'will come at a cost'. Recently, Canada announced retaliatory tariffs on nearly 20 billion dollars’ worth of American goods in response to the US's imposition of duties on Canadian steel and aluminum products. These Canadian levies, which can reach as high as 50%, target a wide range of US products, including steel, aluminum, paper products, food items, and apparel like T-shirts. This latest move reflects a broader trend of countries retaliating against US trade policies, which many leaders warn could hurt the global economy. Carney stressed that trade wars could lead to increased costs for consumers, disruptions in cross-border supply chains, and dampen investment by creating uncertainty. Canadian officials have expressed hope that their targeted measures will compel Washington to reconsider its approach. In response, US authorities have indicated they are prepared to meet these challenges head-on, citing the need to address what they perceive as unfair trade practices. As these tensions ratchet up, markets remain watchful, aware that the trajectory of these disputes holds significant implications for growth and economic stability worldwide.
