Carney calls Trump's fresh tariffs a 'miscalculation' after trade talks collapse

Mark Carney, Governor of the Bank of England, expressed concern over President Donald Trump's decision to impose additional tariffs on Canadian goods, labeling the move a 'miscalculation.' This comes after the recent collapse of trade talks aimed at easing tensions between the two countries. Trump's fresh tariffs were perceived as a heavy-handed attempt to pressure Canada into concessions. In response, Canadian Prime Minister Justin Trudeau announced reluctantly that Canada would impose retaliatory tariffs on American imports. Trudeau emphasized that Canada had been forced into this position and stressed that a trade war was not his country’s desired outcome. Relations between the US and Canada, longtime allies and vital trade partners, seem increasingly strained. Economists are worried about the impact this tit-for-tat tariff war could have on both economies, potentially disrupting supply chains and leading to higher costs for consumers. With trade talks stalled, there are few immediate prospects for resolving tensions. Political analysts suggest that Trump’s tariffs may backfire by uniting Canadian leadership and businesses against US pressure tactics. The situation highlights the complexities of international trade relations in a politically charged environment where economic decisions carry both domestic and international consequences.
