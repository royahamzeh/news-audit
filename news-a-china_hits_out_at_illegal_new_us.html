China hits out at 'illegal' new US sanctions on Iran and trading partners

China has strongly criticized the United States' recent imposition of new sanctions on Iran and its trading partners, labeling the move as 'illegal'. The US has taken a firm stance, warning that countries maintaining trade relations with Tehran, particularly in the oil sector, could face isolation. This threat comes as Iran, struggling under existing sanctions, sells a significant portion of its oil to China. Beijing has condemned Washington's actions, arguing that they violate international norms and agreements. China has been a consistent critic of unilateral sanctions imposed by the US, advocating instead for diplomatic solutions and dialogue. The new sanctions aim to further restrict Iran's oil revenues, limiting Tehran's ability to fund its nuclear program, which the US perceives as a threat. However, by affecting Chinese companies purchasing Iranian oil, the US risks escalating tensions with Beijing during an already precarious period for Sino-US relations. China has reiterated its commitment to maintaining economic ties with Iran, emphasizing the need to adhere to multilateral agreements like the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA). This situation exemplifies the complex web of geopolitical and economic interests at play, highlighting the delicate balance countries must navigate amidst major global powers' competing agendas.
