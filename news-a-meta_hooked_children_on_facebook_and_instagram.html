Meta hooked children on Facebook and Instagram, court hears

In a recently initiated trial, lawyers representing several US states accused Meta Platforms, the parent company of Facebook and Instagram, of deliberately targeting children and adolescents to get them hooked on social media platforms. The trial is the result of a significant lawsuit by these states, alleging that the tech giant employed strategies designed to captivate younger audiences, thus driving up platform engagement at the cost of the users' mental health. As the legal battle unfolds, Meta has taken a firm stance, arguing that the concept of social media addiction lacks scientific evidence and is essentially non-existent. The company’s defense rests on the premise that pointing fingers at social media platforms for addiction diverts attention from broader societal issues. According to Meta's lawyers, the use of platforms like Facebook and Instagram has been overwhelmingly beneficial in connecting people, especially during challenging times. However, the suing states argue otherwise, pointing out potential harm caused by excessive screen time and its adverse effect on children's mental well-being. The outcome of this case could have significant repercussions for how social media is regulated in the future, especially concerning younger users, highlighting the ongoing debate between technological advancement and mental health considerations.
