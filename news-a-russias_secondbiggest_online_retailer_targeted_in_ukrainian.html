Russia's second-biggest online retailer targeted in Ukrainian strikes

Overnight, Ukrainian strikes targeted Russia's second-largest online retailer, Ozon, affecting its warehouses located across southern Russia. Among the regions hit were Dagestan and Krasnodar, where the company reported significant disruptions. Sources close to Ozon revealed the attacks likely aimed at crippling logistics and supply chain operations in a strategic bid to undermine Russian e-commerce infrastructure. Although reports did not specify the extent of the damage, the strikes are believed to have caused notable setbacks for the retailer, which has been expanding its reach across Russia's vast territory. The incident highlights the ongoing tensions between Russia and Ukraine, where economic targets have increasingly become strategic focal points. Ozon, a pivotal player in Russia's online market, finds itself needing to quickly assess the damage and recalibrate operations to avoid long-term impact on its delivery and customer service commitments. This marks one of the rare instances where a civilian corporate entity has been embroiled in the crossfire of military confrontations in the region. As the situation develops, further statements from Ozon and Russian authorities are awaited to provide clarity on the repercussions and the path forward for the company and its numerous stakeholders and customers.
