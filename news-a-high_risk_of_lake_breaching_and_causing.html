'High risk' of lake breaching and causing second flood, warns China

Authorities in China have issued a serious warning about the potential breaching of a newly formed lake following a recent landslide near the Nepal-Tibet border. The incident poses a significant threat of unleashing a second flood in the region, already grappling with the aftermath of the initial landslide. The natural dam, created by debris from the landslide, has partially obstructed the river, leading to the formation of the lake. This accumulation of water is exerting considerable pressure on the already fragile barriers. Experts suggest that if these barriers fail, the resulting flood could cause extensive damage to local communities and infrastructure downstream. In response to the looming threat, the authorities have mobilized emergency teams to monitor the lake's levels closely and are preparing evacuation plans for nearby residents. Local governments have also been urged to organize resources and be ready for immediate action should the situation deteriorate. This development has sparked concerns among environmental groups about the increasing incidence of natural disasters in the area, potentially linked to climate change. The region's frail topography, coupled with changing climate patterns, underscores the urgent need for comprehensive disaster risk management strategies to mitigate such threats in the future.
